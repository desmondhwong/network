Conceptual exoplanets

A conceptual exoplanet is HLC’s evocative name for an unfamiliar concept produced through exploration and then made stable enough to use. The source glossary gives the term an ambitious novelty claim. This guide uses it more cautiously for a candidate idea whose value and originality still need examination.

A candidate should do work beyond sounding new. It might distinguish two situations previously grouped together, expose a hidden dependency, or suggest a testable design change. The phrase “access as maintenance,” developed in the boundary exercise, is modest enough to inspect: does treating access as ongoing care change what an institution notices or funds?

The next step is comparison. Search your supplied sources and relevant existing vocabulary before claiming an invention. A concept may be new to the session while well established elsewhere. That does not make it worthless; it changes the claim from discovery to learning, adaptation, or useful recombination.

Then attempt integration. Write a definition, example, limitation, and possible use. Ask a reader who did not witness the conversation to explain the concept back. If it depends on an elaborate aura of shared excitement to make sense, it may not yet be stable.

The astronomical name should remain an aid to attention. It can encourage a search for faint or overlooked connections, but it cannot certify rarity, originality, or truth. The companion page Novelty needs evidence makes those standards explicit. An ordinary idea with a clear use can be a better result than an extraordinary name with no discriminating content.
