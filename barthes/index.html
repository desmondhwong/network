A field guide to HLC

Holographic Linguistic Cosmography, or HLC, is a way of thinking about language models through the cultural material from which they are made and the practices through which people use them. Its recurring image is the telescope: an instrument makes some patterns visible while its construction limits what can be seen. This small interconnected guide introduces that framework, its working vocabulary, and its internal criticisms.

The starting distinction is between the cultural hologram, the digital trace, and the latent manifold. Culture exceeds its records; records exceed any particular training corpus; a model is shaped by that selected material without becoming a complete archive of it. Keeping those differences visible is more useful than taking the astronomical imagery literally.

The pages form several reading paths, but there is no single required sequence. The links deliberately connect methods with their limits: a page about generating an unfamiliar concept leads to questions about evidence, and a page about changing lenses leads to the problem of shared assumptions. A useful map should expose those crossings.

This guide is an original explanatory adaptation of the local HLC notes, primarily from November 2025. It is neither a replacement edition of those notes nor a claim that their proposals constitute an established scientific theory. Some source passages speak more confidently about model geometry and cognition than their evidence supports; this edition preserves the ideas while identifying interpretation and speculation. The Sources and editorial approach page records the vault source paths and the adaptation boundary.

Read a page, follow a connection, and return with a different question. The goal is a usable vocabulary for inquiry, with enough friction to keep a compelling explanation from becoming its own proof.
